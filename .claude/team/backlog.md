## Specs for new items

Every spec: copy a neighbour calculator, add `examples` with the hand-checked values below, make FAQ answers match `compute()`, and keep the link to `related` calculators.

1. **Turkey time** (`food`). Inputs: weight (lb, with kg toggle), stuffed yes/no, oven 325 F, fresh or frozen-thawed, serve time. Formula: use the USDA FSIS roasting table at 325 F (unstuffed: 8-12 lb 2.75-3 h, 12-14 lb 3-3.75 h, 14-18 lb 3.75-4.25 h, 18-20 lb 4.25-4.5 h, 20-24 lb 4.5-5 h; copy the stuffed table from the same page, I only saw secondary charts, so verify every row). Output: cook-time range, start time = serve time - time - 30 min rest, and fridge thaw days = ceil(lb/5) to ceil(lb/4) (USDA: 24 h per 4-5 lb, verify). Safe temp 165 F (thigh, breast and stuffing). Example: 16 lb unstuffed -> 3.75-4.25 h; serve 17:00 with a 30-min rest -> out of the oven 16:30, so in the oven 12:15 (4.25 h) to 12:45 (3.75 h). Thaw 16 lb in the fridge -> 4 days (ceil(16/5)=4, ceil(16/4)=4). Sources: USDA FSIS "Roasting turkey" and "Big Bird" thawing pages.
2. **Stacked discounts** (improvement to `finance/discount`). Add: price, discount 1 %, extra discount 2 % (applied after), coupon $ off, sales tax %. Formula: price x (1-d1) x (1-d2) - coupon, then tax. Output: total off % = 1 - (1-d1)(1-d2). Example: $200, 30% then 20% -> $112.00, 44% off, not 50%. Add a social line "30% + 20% is 44%, not 50%".
3. **Gift budget** (`finance`). Inputs: total budget, fixed costs (travel, wrapping), adults, kids, kid weight (default 1.0, optional 1.5, note it is a rule of thumb from one site, not a standard), weeks left, optional already spent. Formula: per adult = (total - fixed) / (adults + kids x weight); per kid = per adult x weight; weekly saving = remaining / weeks. Example: $1,000, $200 fixed, 2 adults, 4 kids at 1.5 -> 800 / 8 = $100 per adult, $150 per kid. Benchmark NRF holiday figures only if linked to NRF itself (sources I saw disagree: $890 vs $628 gifts only).
4. **Savings challenge** (`finance`). Inputs: start amount, weekly increase, weeks (default 52), direction (up/down). Formula: sum of n terms = n/2 x (2a + (n-1)d). Example: $1 start, $1 step, 52 weeks -> $1,378; week 52 deposit $52. Chart: cumulative savings. Optional: add interest-free note.
5. **Rule of 72** (`finance`). Inputs: rate % or target years. Output: 72/rate years (approx) and exact ln 2 / ln(1+r). Example: 8% -> 9.0 years (exact 9.006); 6% -> 12 (exact 11.9). Say 72 is accurate for 6-10%.
6. **Subscription cost** (`finance`). Inputs: up to 10 rows (name, price, monthly/yearly/weekly). Formula: annual = monthly x 12, weekly x 52, yearly x 1. Optional: invest the savings at 7% for 10 years (annuity-immediate, assumption labelled). Example: $10 + $15 + $5 monthly -> $30/month, $360/year; a $120 yearly plan adds $120 -> $480/year, $40/month.
7. **Overtime pay** (`everyday`). Inputs: hourly rate, hours worked, weekly threshold 40, multiplier 1.5, optional holiday hours at 2x. Formula: regular = min(h,40) x rate; OT = max(h-40,0) x rate x mult. Example: $20, 48 h -> 800 + 8 x 30 = $1,040. Note state rules (e.g. daily overtime in CA) and link US DOL FLSA overtime page.
8. **Party food** (`food`). Inputs: guests (adults, kids), meal type. Formula: per-person amounts from a cited table (Butterball suggests about 1-1.5 lb of turkey per person, verify). Example: 10 guests x 1.5 lb -> 15 lb turkey. Add sides in a table; every constant must have a source before merge.
9. **Bill splitter** (`everyday`). Inputs: people with item subtotals, tax $ (or %), tip % (on pre-tax subtotal toggle). Formula: each person's share of tax and tip is proportional to subtotal. Example: A $30, B $20, C $50 (sum $100), tax $8, tip 20% of pre-tax = $20 -> total $128; A $38.40, B $25.60, C $64.00.
10. **Steps to distance** (`health`). Inputs: steps, height, sex. Formula: stride = height x 0.415 (men) or 0.413 (women), a common rule of thumb (cite and label as approximate). Example: 10,000 steps, 175 cm, male -> stride 72.6 cm, 7.26 km, 4.51 mi. Cross-link `calories-burned`.
11. **Plate calculator** (`sports`). Inputs: target weight, bar weight (45 lb or 20 kg), available plates, unit. Formula: per side = (target - bar)/2, greedy from largest plate; flag if not exact. Example: 225 lb, 45 lb bar -> 90 per side = 2 x 45. 135 lb -> 45 per side = one 45.
12. **Tax-loss harvesting** (`finance`). Inputs: realised gains this year, harvestable loss, filing status, ordinary and capital-gains rates. Formula: net = gains - loss; losses offset gains first, then up to $3,000 of ordinary income ($1,500 if married filing separately), rest carries forward (IRC 1211(b); verify on IRS Topic 409). Example: $4,000 gains, $10,000 loss, 24% ordinary, 15% LTCG -> gains tax saved 4,000 x 15% = $600; $3,000 x 24% = $720 deducted; $3,000 carryforward. Add wash-sale warning (30 days).
13. **Heating cost** (`everyday`). Inputs: heater watts, hours per day, $/kWh, furnace gas $/therm, AFUE. Formula: electric heat = kWh x 3,412 BTU; gas cost for the same heat = (BTU / 100,000) / AFUE x $/therm. Example: 1,500 W x 8 h = 12 kWh = 40,944 BTU; at $0.17/kWh cost $2.04; gas at $1.50/therm and AFUE 0.95 -> $0.65 (ratio about 3.2x). Note: a space heater only saves money if the thermostat is lowered. Sources: EIA, DOE Energy Saver. Differs from existing `electricity-cost` and `gas-cost`: compare two heat sources.
14. **Secret Santa** (`other`). Inputs: names (2-30), optional "cannot draw" pairs. Output: a derangement (nobody draws themself), shown one at a time with a reveal so the organiser does not see all. No storage, no sharing. Example check: 3 names -> only the two 3-cycles are valid; 2 names -> A to B, B to A. Test property: no fixed points.
15. **Fuel economy** (`conversion`). Formula: L/100km = 235.215 / mpg (US); km/L = 100 / (L/100km). Example: 30 mpg -> 7.84 L/100km -> 12.75 km/L. Add UK mpg (282.481 / mpg).
16. **Time zone converter** (`everyday`). Inputs: date, time, from zone, to zone (IANA list via `Intl.DateTimeFormat`). Output: converted time, day shift, DST note. Example: 2026-12-24 12:00 America/New_York -> 17:00 Europe/London (GMT, no DST). Check examples in tests without relying on the test machine's locale.
17. **chmod** (`developer`). Inputs: checkbox grid r/w/x for user, group, other, optional setuid/gid/sticky. Output: octal and symbolic. Example: 755 = rwxr-xr-x; 644 = rw-r--r--; 600 = rw-------.
18. **Stair calculator** (`construction`). Inputs: total rise, target riser height, tread depth. Formula: risers = round(rise / target); riser = rise / risers; treads = risers - 1; run = treads x tread. Example: rise 108 in, target 7.5 -> 14 risers (14.4 rounded), 7.71 in each, 13 treads, run at 10 in = 130 in. Check against IRC R311.7 (max riser 7.75 in, min tread 10 in; verify current text).
19. **Latte factor** (`finance`). Inputs: cost per purchase, purchases per week, years, return % (default 7, labelled an assumption), contribution timing. Formula: annual spend = cost x per week x 52; future value = annual x ((1+r)^n - 1) / r (end of year). Example: $5 x 7 x 52 = $1,820 a year (use 365 days -> $1,825); at 7% for 30 years with $1,825 -> $172,391 (1.07^30 = 7.612255). Show spent total $54,750 vs growth. Say plainly it ignores tax, fees and inflation, and that one coffee is not the reason anyone cannot retire. Cross-link `compound-interest`, `subscription-cost`. Sources: standard annuity formula; the "latte factor" is a popularised idea, do not claim a $2 million figure.
20. **Gym cost per visit** (`health`). Inputs: monthly fee, joining fee, months, visits per month, drop-in price. Formula: cost per visit = (fee x months + joining) / (visits x months); break-even visits per month = fee / drop-in. Example: $60 a month, 10 visits -> $6.00 per visit; drop-in $15 -> 60/15 = 4 visits a month. With $40 joining over 12 months and 10 visits a month: (720 + 40) / 120 = $6.33. Share line: "my gym costs $X a visit, i'm cooked". Do not cite unused-membership statistics unless linked to a primary source.
21. **Raise calculator** (`finance`). Inputs: current salary, raise as % or new salary, hours per week (default 40), inflation % (optional), combined marginal tax rate % (optional, user supplied; note it is an estimate). Formula: raise $ = salary x %; monthly = /12; weekly = /52; hourly = salary / (hours x 52); real raise % = (1+raise)/(1+inflation) - 1; after-tax raise = raise $ x (1 - marginal rate). Example: $60,000 at 5% -> $63,000, +$3,000 a year, $250 a month, $57.69 a week; hourly $28.85 -> $30.29 (2,080 h); at 3.3% inflation real raise = 1.05/1.033 - 1 = 1.65%; at 22% federal + 7.65% FICA = 29.65% marginal, after-tax raise = $2,110.50. Link `take-home-pay`, `inflation`, `percentage-change`.
22. **Habit compounding** (`math`). Inputs: daily change % (default 1), days (default 365), direction better/worse. Formula: (1 +/- r)^days. Example: 1.01^365 = 37.78; 0.99^365 = 0.0255 (2.6% left); 1.01^30 = 1.348 (the "only 35% better after a month" point). Chart: curve with a linear-growth line (365% -> 4.65x) for contrast. State that this is a metaphor for effort, not a model of real skill gains.
23. **Year progress** (`everyday`). Inputs: date (default today). Output: day of year, % of year elapsed (day / 365 or 366), days left, weeks left, weekdays left, days to Jan 1. Example: 2026-12-16 -> 16 days to 2027-01-01 (15 left in December plus Jan 1); 2026-12-31 is day 365 of 365 = 100%. Must differ from `date-difference` (presets and the shareable "year is X% done" card). Test with fixed dates, not the machine clock.
24. **NYE party drinks** (`food`). Inputs: guests who drink, hours, drinks per hour model, beer/wine/spirits/bubbly split, non-drinkers. Formula (verify against https://www.omnicalculator.com/food/party-drink before use): first hour 2 drinks, later hours 1 per hour, add 15% buffer; 1 bottle of wine (750 ml) = 5 glasses of 150 ml. Example: 20 drinkers, 5 hours -> 2 + 4 x 1 = 6 drinks each = 120; +15% = 138; if 40% wine = 55.2 drinks -> 11.04 -> 12 bottles. Add a standard-drink note (US 14 g alcohol, NIAAA) and a "no drinking and driving" line. Every constant needs a source.
25. **Christmas lights** (`everyday`). Inputs: strings, watts per string, hours per day, days, $/kWh; second mode: circuit amps (15 or 20), volts 120, other load. Formula: cost = W x h x days / 1,000 x rate; circuit limit = volts x amps x 0.8 (continuous load). Example: 10 strings, 400 W incandescent vs 70 W LED total, 6 h, 45 days, $0.165/kWh -> 108 kWh = $17.82 vs 18.9 kWh = $3.12. Circuit: 120 V x 15 A = 1,800 W, 80% = 1,440 W continuous. Sources: DOE Energy Saver (verify), NEC 80% rule (verify). Note retailer pages disagree on per-string watts, so make watts an input with no hidden defaults beyond labelled examples. Differs from `electricity-cost`: lights-specific presets and circuit check.
26. **Gas-cost extras** (improvement). Add optional tolls, lodging per night x nights, food per day, and show total trip cost and per person. Example: 500 mi round trip at 28 mpg and $3.50 -> 17.857 gal x 3.50 = $62.50 fuel (search page rounded to $63); add $10 tolls -> $72.50; split 2 -> $36.25 each. Keep existing outputs unchanged so `examples` still pass.
27. **Wind chill** (`everyday`). Inputs: temperature, wind speed, unit toggle. Formula (NWS 2001, US units): 35.74 + 0.6215T - 35.75 V^0.16 + 0.4275 T V^0.16, valid only for T at or below 50 F and V above 3 mph; show "not defined" otherwise. Example: 0 F, 15 mph -> -19.4 F (15^0.16 = 1.5423). Convert metric inputs to F and mph first; do not use the Canadian coefficients unless sourced (sources I saw conflict). Source: https://www.weather.gov/ama/WindChill . Note sunshine can raise the effective value.
