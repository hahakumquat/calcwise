## Reddit (r/personalfinance, as an answer in a thread asking what an early 401(k) or IRA withdrawal really costs, not a standalone post)
No image. Re-read the current r/personalfinance rules before posting; the sub restricts self-promotion and calculator links, so the safest route is the math with no link at all. Add the "I made this" line only if the current rules and the thread clearly allow it.

"The math is simple, so you can check it yourself. Cash kept = withdrawal - (taxable part x penalty rate) - (taxable part x your marginal tax rate) - state tax. Example with made-up rates: $10,000 from a traditional 401(k), 10% additional tax, 22% federal, 5% state. Penalty $1,000, federal $2,200, state $500, total $3,700, so you keep $6,300 (37% gone). A few things change it: the withdrawal stacks on top of your other income so the top slice can land in a higher bracket, some withdrawals have exceptions, and Roth contributions are treated differently from earnings, so check the IRS page on early distributions or ask a tax pro for your case. Disclosure: I made a free calculator that does this sum, in case it helps: https://cappycalc.com/finance/early-withdrawal-penalty/"
