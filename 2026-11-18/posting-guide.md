## Reddit (r/mortgages, as an answer in a thread asking if buying points is worth it, not a standalone post)
No image. Re-read r/mortgages' current rules before posting; lead with the math, and add the link only with the "I made this" line, or skip the link.

"The way to compare is break-even: cost of the points divided by the monthly payment saving. Example with made-up but typical numbers: $300,000 loan, 30 years, 7% with no points. One point costs 1% of the loan, $3,000. If it cuts the rate by 0.25% to 6.75%, the payment drops from about $1,995.91 to $1,945.79, a saving of $50.11 a month. $3,000 / $50.11 is about 59.9 months, so it breaks even a bit under 5 years in. If you keep the loan 10 years, the points save about $3,014 net (120 x $50.11 - $3,000). That ignores what the $3,000 could earn elsewhere, and the real rate cut per point varies by lender, so use the number on your loan estimate. This is just the arithmetic, not a recommendation. Disclosure: I made a free calculator that runs this, if useful: https://cappycalc.com/finance/mortgage-points/"

Best time: inside a live thread, weekday 8 to 10 am local.
