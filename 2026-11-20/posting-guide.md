## Reddit (r/personalfinance, as an answer in a thread asking how to compare a pay-later plan to paying upfront, not a standalone post)
No image. Re-read the current r/personalfinance rules before posting; they restrict self-promotion, so the safest route is the math with no link. Add the "I made this" line only if the rules and thread clearly allow it.

"Easiest way to compare is to add up everything you pay and subtract the price. Example with made-up numbers: $1,200 purchase, 12 equal monthly payments, 12% APR, no fees. Monthly rate is 1%, so the payment is about $106.62. Times 12 is $1,279.42, so the plan costs about $79.42 extra, roughly 6.6% of the price. If a plan is 0% APR but charges a flat $50 fee on $1,000 over 12 months, the payments are $83.33 and the extra is $50, so 5%. Late fees would be on top of either. The monthly number looking small tells you nothing about the extra, so it helps to look at the total. This is just arithmetic with example numbers, your agreement's real terms are what count. Disclosure: I made a free calculator that does this, in case it helps: https://cappycalc.com/finance/bnpl-true-cost/"

Best time: inside a live thread, weekday 8 to 10 am local.
